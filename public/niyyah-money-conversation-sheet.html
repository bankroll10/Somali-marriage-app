The money conversation

A sheet for two adults considering marriage, to work through before the families discuss numbers. Each of you answers on your own phone, and neither sees the other’s answers until you have both finished.

The four subjects are kept apart on purpose. Mahr is not the wedding. The wedding is not a debt either of you already carries. And the money you send to family every month, at home or abroad, is a subject of its own.

Nothing on this page is saved or sent. What you type stays in this window until you close it, and there is nothing to submit.

Saved only on this device

What you type is saved in this browser on this device, so you can close the page and come back to it. Nothing on this page is sent anywhere, and the PDF is made here too.

Saved answers are removed from this device 14 days after the last change. On a shared or public device, tap Clear my answers when you finish.

This browser is not letting the page save. Download the PDF before you close or reload it, or your answers will be lost.

How to use this

  1. Each of you opens this page on your own phone and chooses Person 1 or Person 2. It does not matter who is which.
  2. Answer on your own, without showing each other. It takes about 30 to 40 minutes, and you can skip any question.
  3. Tap Finish at the end. Send the link it gives you to your partner by text, or save the file and send that.
  4. When your partner’s link or file arrives, open it on this page. You get one PDF with both of your answers side by side, and a first page listing what to talk about.
  5. Bring that PDF to your session with the imam.

Sharing one phone? The first person taps Finish, saves the file, then taps Clear my answers so the second person can start.

Also: the one-page version, to print a blank copy and write on it by hand, and in Somali.

Who is filling this in?

Choose one. Your partner chooses the other.

Your name and the date are optional. They are printed on the PDF, and your name goes above your answers.

Section 1 of 4

1. Mahr

This section is about mahr only — what each of you expects, and who has heard that expectation so far.

Have the two of you discussed the mahr amount yet?

  1. What do you expect the mahr to be? Write it in your own words, in whatever form you expect it.

    Digits only. Use the same currency as your partner.

  2. Who have you said that to so far — your partner, your own family, anyone else?

  3. How much of it do you expect at the nikah, and how much deferred?

    Choose the closest

  4. If your family names something different from what you just wrote, what would you want to happen next?

  5. Who speaks for you when that conversation happens, and have you asked them?

Section 2 of 4

2. The wedding — one-time expenses

This section is about the one-time cost of the wedding itself — the nikah, the walima, and whatever either side expects around them. It is separate from mahr.

Who do you expect to pay for most of the wedding?

  1. Which parts of the wedding do you expect to pay for yourself?

  2. Which parts do you expect someone else to pay for, and have you asked them?

  3. Of the people who would be there, how many are there because of you?

  4. What is the most you are willing to spend in total? Write your own ceiling, not a guess at what it will cost.

    Digits only. Use the same currency as your partner.

  5. If the cost goes past that ceiling, what is the first thing you would cut — and what would you cut last?

Section 3 of 4

3. Debt either of you already carries

This section is about money either of you already owes, from before anything about this marriage was decided.

Do you have any debts right now?

  1. What do you owe right now, and to whom? Include anything that has someone else’s name on it.

  2. What are you paying toward it each month, and how long is left?

  3. Who already knows about it — your partner, your own family, your partner’s family?

  4. After the marriage, do you expect this to stay yours, or to be something you both handle?

    Choose the closest

  5. Is there anything here you would want settled before the nikah rather than after?

Section 4 of 4

4. Ongoing obligations to family

This section is about money that leaves your account regularly for people outside this marriage — parents, siblings, relatives here or abroad.

Do you send money to family regularly?

  1. Who do you send money to now, and how often?

  2. What does that come to, against what you earn?

  3. Is the amount fixed, or does it change when something happens?

  4. Who knows what you send — your partner, your own family, the people you send it to?

  5. After the marriage, do you expect this to stay the same, go up, or come down — and if yours and your partner’s are different sizes, how do you want that handled?
