# The reality sprint — the research protocol for the first ten

*2026-09-12; updated 2026-09-24. Read once before session one; carried into
every session as the checklist and the questions; never reinterpreted.*

## Context

Zero real people have used Niyyah. The hypothesis: **Niyyah is valuable as
the decision layer between "we're talking" and "we're getting married", with
no marketplace behind it** (cut 2026-09-24; the decision layer is the whole
product). So the sample leans toward people currently talking to someone,
with singles as contrast, and the one behavioural outcome that outranks
every opinion is: **did Niyyah cause a conversation that otherwise probably
would not have happened?** That is the North Star in one person
(`docs/PRODUCT.md`: followed-through per hundred arrived).

Everything below is anchored to the product as it is on `main` today and to
the questions the documents already committed to asking (`docs/RESEARCH.md`:
the ten conversations, the three bins, A3 and A5).

**No code moved for this.** The protocol is a document; the product it tests
is the one on `main`. Sessions are logged in `docs/RESEARCH.md` using the
template below, and the decision rules here are executed, not debated
(`docs/RESEARCH.md`'s rule).

---

## WHO TO RECRUIT

Somali diaspora, 24–36, in one metro if possible (Minneapolis–St Paul
first). People the founder can sit beside for half an hour, or hold on a
video call while they hold their own phone. Not first-degree family, not
anyone the founder is or has been romantically connected to, and at least
three not from the founder's own close circle — a friend's friend, someone
from a connector's room.

Recruit through the ten connectors and the founder's own network with one
message:

> *Salaam. I'm testing something I've been building for Somali people who are
> talking to someone, or thinking about marriage — before anyone else sees it.
> I need 25 minutes on your phone, in person or on a call, to watch where it
> goes wrong. I'm not looking for compliments; I'm looking for what's off. No
> account, no name, nothing you type comes to me. Would you give me half an
> hour this week?*

Do not describe the product in the recruiting message beyond that. Do not
send the link ahead. Ask them to come with the phone they use every day, not
in a private browsing window (the product keeps its state on the phone, and
the three-day follow-up needs it to still be there).

## HOW MANY OF EACH TYPE

Ten sessions. Deliberately mixed, not representative.

| # | Who | Side | Why they are in the sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Currently talking to someone** (weeks to months in, families not yet formally involved) | women | The primary hypothesis. The read is for them tonight; the eleven is for them next month |
| 2 | **Currently talking to someone** | men | The side the product had never been read from until 2026-09-11. Whether a man comes for himself or only answers her eleven is the men's gap in `docs/RESEARCH.md` |
| 1 | **Recently ended** — a serious talking that stopped inside the last year | woman | The retrospective test: what became difficult, what was found out too late. Answers the read's premise from the far side |
| 1 | **Recently ended** | man | Same, from his side |
| 1 | **Preparing / single**, wants to marry, not talking to anyone | woman | Contrast. If the instruments feel like nothing to her, the decision-layer hypothesis strengthens. If she finds real value, the read-alone thesis has legs too |
| 1 | **Preparing / single** | man | Contrast, his side |

Six women, four men. Six currently talking, two recently ended, two single.
Of the six currently talking, **at least two must be people whose counterpart
is reachable that week**, so the eleven's send can be tested for
real rather than hypothetically.

At least eight of ten Somali. Up to two may be non-Somali Muslim diaspora
(Pakistani, Arab, other East African) — deliberately, as the specificity
control: `docs/RESEARCH.md` conviction 1 needs to know which of the eleven a
non-Somali friend would also need. If recruiting them is slow, run all ten
Somali and do not wait.

## THE EXACT 20–30 MINUTE SESSION

Run in this order. The past comes **before** the phone, so the product cannot
anchor what they remember.

| Min | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | **Frame** | The script under "What I say" below, word for word. Phone stays in their pocket |
| 2–8 | **Their past, first** | Four questions from "What I ask" section A. Notes only. No product yet |
| 8–9 | **The link** | Send the link to their phone by WhatsApp or SMS while sitting there. Talking or ended → `https://joinniyyah.com/?read` · Single → `https://joinniyyah.com/?read` framed as "the last person you seriously talked to" · Anyone in a marriage conversation with families involved → `https://joinniyyah.com/?eleven` instead. No `via` — sessions land as `unsaid`, and the founder knows the first ten `unsaid` arrivals are these |
| 9–19 | **The phone, silently** | They think aloud; the founder says nothing except "keep going" and "what do you think it wants?" The observation checklist runs the whole time |
| 19–22 | **The second instrument, if there is time** | If they finish the read with time left and are talking to someone: "There's one more thing on that screen you might send someone. Have a look." Nothing more. Watch whether they find the eleven and what they do at "Ask her / him to do this too" |
| 22–28 | **Afterward** | Sections B–E of "What I ask", in order, skipping what the session already answered |
| 28–30 | **The send moment** | If they are talking to someone: "You can send that to them right now, from here, if you want to. You don't have to." Then silence. Record what they do. Then: "Can I text you on Thursday to ask one question?" |
| +3 days | **The follow-up** | One text, three questions (section F). This is where the outcome lives |

Twenty-five minutes is the target. Thirty is the ceiling; if they are still
mid-read at twenty-two, let them finish and cut section D.

## WHAT I SAY BEFORE HANDING THEM THE PHONE

Say this, close to word for word, then stop talking:

> *Thank you for doing this. I built something and I need to find out where
> it's wrong before anyone else sees it — so I'm not going to explain it, and
> I'm not going to help you. If you get stuck, that's my mistake, not yours,
> and it's exactly what I need to see. Please talk out loud as you go: what
> you're thinking, what you expect to happen, what's confusing, what's
> annoying. Be blunt. Nothing you type comes to me with your name on it — it
> stays on your phone unless you choose otherwise. One part of it, if you
> reach it, uses an AI service and the screen tells you so before you use it;
> you can leave that alone. One more honest thing: the other people trying
> this are from the same community, so you may know some of them — nothing
> here shows a name or a face, but I want you to know that going in. You can
> stop at any time. Before I send you the link, I want to ask you a few things
> about your own experience.*

Then section A. Then the link. Then silence.

## WHAT I MUST NOT EXPLAIN

The product must carry itself, because in the wild it will have to.

- What "the read" is, what it measures, or what "serious" means here.
- That the eleven exist, or that they can be sent to the other person.
- Which button does what. Where "back" is. Where the words are.
- What a "stage" is, or that it was inferred for them.
- What the Trust page says, or that one exists.
- What the guide is, whether to use it, or what it sends.
- That they can keep their map.
- Any Somali word or line — if they ask what it means, ask what they think.
- Why a question is asked the way it is. If they object to a question, note
  the objection; do not defend it.
- Anything about the founder's intentions, the business, or the "vision".

If they ask "what do I do now?", the only permitted answer is *"What do you
think it wants you to do?"* If they are stuck for more than thirty
seconds, say *"Do whatever you'd do if I weren't here"* — and record it as
confusion. Never touch their phone.

## WHAT I OBSERVE SILENTLY

A tick-and-timestamp list, one per session. Every line is something the
readout can never show.

**The first thirty seconds**
- Which side they pick at the start, read or eleven — and any pause.
- Whether they read the intro sentence ("Twelve questions about what he has
  done…") or skip it. Whether they react to the word "done".

**The twelve questions of the read**
- Any question they re-read, or pause on for more than five seconds. Which.
- Any question they answer and then change.
- Any question that makes them laugh, wince, or say "hmm". Which, and which.
- Whether any answer makes them defensive of the other person ("well, he
  did…").
- Whether they narrate the other person's behaviour while answering — that
  is the read working.
- Any moment they look up at the founder for permission or reassurance.

**The result**
- Their face at the headline. Their first words.
- Whether they read the summary aloud, silently, or skim.
- Whether they scroll to the words ("The one question to ask next") and
  whether they read the words **aloud, unprompted**. That is the strongest
  single signal in the session.
- Whether they tap "Copy the words". Whether they tap "Send these words to
  someone who needs them" — and stop, or go through.
- Whether they find the "Before you say yes" card and what they do with it.
- Whether they open Trust, the guide, or the family scripts unprompted.
- Whether any pronoun, name, or gendered phrase makes them flinch.
- Whether they hit "Take the read again" — and why, if they say.

**The eleven, if reached**
- Which of the eleven topics they slow down on. Which they say "we've never
  talked about that" at, aloud.
- Which get "I don't know my own answer yet". That is the product's most
  interesting answer and the one to ask about after.
- Their face at "Ask her / him to do this too". Whether they read the
  sentence about frozen answers. Whether they tap. Whether they stop at the
  moment the link would be sent.

**Somali-specific lines**
- Any Somali sentence on screen: read aloud, smiled at, skipped, winced at,
  mispronounced-and-laughed. Which line.
- Any English line with a Somali word in it — hooyo, wali, qabiil, aroos,
  mahr, dugsi — that draws a reaction.

**Exit**
- Where they stop. Whether they stop early, and on which screen.
- Whether they say anything before the founder asks.

## WHAT I ASK AFTERWARD

Behaviour and specific past events. Never "do you like it", "would you use
it", or "is this a good idea". If they answer a "would" question, redirect
to the last time.

The jobs in `docs/PRODUCT.md` are the hypothesis sheet these answers are
read against: each stage's situation, jobs, alternatives, anxieties and habit,
and every feature's claimed job. Question 4 fills its *alternatives* column; 7
and 15 test the functional and social jobs; the three-day text is the outcome.

**A · Before the phone (min 2–8)**
1. *The last person you seriously talked to about marriage — how did it start,
   and how long did it go on?* (If single: *the last time you seriously wanted
   to meet someone — what did you do, and who knew?* —
   `docs/RESEARCH.md`, the ten conversations)
2. *What became difficult? When did you first notice, and when did you say
   something?*
3. *What did you find out about them, or about yourselves, that you wish you
   had found out earlier? How did it come out?*
4. *What have you used, or tried, to meet someone or to figure out someone
   you were talking to — apps, aunties, friends, nothing? What happened, and
   why did it stop working: nobody there, you couldn't trust who was there,
   or it didn't understand your situation?* (`docs/RESEARCH.md` — the three
   bins; record the bin)

**B · The read (min 22–24)**
5. *Which question was hardest to answer honestly? Why?*
6. *Was anything in what it told you something that had not already occurred
   to you? What, exactly?* (If nothing: *what did it say that you already
   knew?* — that answer is data too)
7. *The words it gave you to say. Read them back to me in your own voice.* —
   then: *Would you say that, to them, this week? What would you
   change first?* (Any change they make, write down word for word)
8. *If you'd had this the last time — at which point in that story would you
   have opened it, and what would you have done differently?*

**C · The eleven (if reached; min 24–26)**
9. *Of the eleven, which had you two talked about? Which surprised
   you by being on the list? Which is missing?*
10. *Would you send that to them — the real person — from your own phone?
    What would you type above the link when you did?* (Then the send moment
    at min 28. What they type is the invitation copy the product should
    have.)
11. *Which of these would a Pakistani or Arab friend of yours also need, and
    which are only ours?* (`docs/RESEARCH.md` conviction 1 — portability)

**D · The words and the culture (min 26–27)**
12. *Was anything on those screens said in a way that felt false, exaggerated,
    embarrassing, or like an auntie? Anything that sounded like it was
    written by someone outside the community? Anything written the wrong way
    round for a man / for a woman?* — go line by line if they hesitate.
13. (Men only) *Did any of it feel like it was written for a woman and
    flipped? Where?*
14. *Any Somali sentence you saw — how did it land? Would you say it that
    way?*

**E · Trust and the family (min 27–28)**
15. *At any point did you wonder who sees this? What made you trust it or
    not — the words, the look, who sent it, something else?*
16. *Who in your family knows you're talking to someone? Who would you want
    to know before it goes further, and how would you tell them?*
17. *At the last wedding in your family, was anyone paid for bringing the two
    of them together — who paid, how much, and when?* (`docs/RESEARCH.md`
    conviction 7, word for word, and A5. Amounts, never
    names.)
18. *What would you never want a screenshot of from this?* (`docs/RESEARCH.md`)

**F · Three days later, by text**
> *Salaam — one question from the other day. The thing it gave you to say, or
> the conversation it pointed at: did you have it? Reply with one of three —
> "we talked", "not yet", or "it went differently" — and a line on what came
> back or what got in the way. (And did you send them the link?)*

Three answers, one message, no chasing. They are the product's own three
(`src/components/home/FollowUp.tsx`); only "we talked" counts as followed
through, exactly as `src/lib/followup.ts` counts it. A non-reply is a data
point and is recorded as one.

## WHAT I RECORD

Per session, on paper or a notes app that never touches the repository,
then transcribed the same day into `docs/RESEARCH.md` in the template below.
No names, no phone numbers, no employer, no neighbourhood finer than the
metro. The counterpart is "them", never a name, never a description that
would identify them. The founder keeps one offline paper sheet mapping
session number → how to reach them for the Thursday text, and destroys it
after the follow-up.

What gets written for every session, whether or not it was interesting:

- Session id `S01`–`S10`, date, side, stage bin (talking / ended / single),
  Somali or control, how recruited (connector room / friend-of-friend /
  founder's circle), in person or call.
- Section A answers, paraphrased, with the three-bin verdict.
- The observation checklist: which questions paused on, the face at the
  result, words read aloud or not, copy/send tapped or not, eleven reached
  or not, Somali lines' reception, where they exited.
- Section B–E answers, paraphrased. Any words they would change, verbatim.
  Anything they said was new to them, verbatim.
- Every item classified: **bug / confusion / cultural failure / real value /
  polite praise** — using the definitions below, not the founder's mood.
- The send moment: did not / hesitated / sent.
- Thursday: replied or not; said the words or not; which conversation, if
  any, happened; sent the link or not.
- Bears on: the gap, experiment id (A-), conviction or ledger row (L-) in
  `docs/RESEARCH.md` it speaks to, or `new`.

## WHAT COUNTS AS A BUG

Something the product did that it was not built to do. Fix before the next
session; a bug in session 3 invalidates sessions 4–10 if left.

- Anything that crashes, blanks, hangs, or shows the recovery screen.
- A tap that does nothing, or does the wrong thing.
- Text that names the wrong side — "he" about a woman, "she" about a man,
  "sister" to a man — anywhere on any screen.
- A back button that lands somewhere other than where they came from.
- State that vanishes: they finish the read, close the tab, reopen, and it
  is gone.
- A layout that clips, overlaps, or needs horizontal scrolling on their
  phone.
- The guide returning `{}` or an error string as a reply, or nothing.
- The eleven's send producing a link that does not open on the other phone.

## WHAT COUNTS AS CONFUSION

The product working as built and the person not understanding it. Recorded
every time; changed only if two or more people hit the same one.

- Asking "what do I do?" or looking up for help — on which screen.
- More than thirty seconds still on a screen with no tap.
- Answering a read question about themselves when it asks about the other
  person, or vice versa.
- Not realising the result has words to say on it. Not finding "Copy".
- Not understanding that the eleven can be sent, or believing the other
  person would see their answers.
- Misreading a stage label or a chip.
- Asking what a Somali or Arabic word on screen means.
- Thinking they have to make an account, or looking for a login.
- Believing something was sent to the founder that was not.

## WHAT COUNTS AS A CULTURAL FAILURE

A line that a Somali person of the target age says is wrong about us. The
most expensive class, and the one no test could catch. One person saying it
is a flag; two people saying it independently is a verdict. A verdict about a
line in `docs/RESEARCH.md`'s evidence ledger is class D evidence on its row:
name the L-id under "Bears on".

- **Outsider-ish.** "Nobody would say it like that." "That's how a non-Somali
  would describe us." A Somali word used wrongly, or explained as if to a
  stranger.
- **Auntie-ish.** Moralising, hectoring, "you should", a tone that treats a
  27-year-old as a child. Anything that sounds like a lecture at a wedding.
- **Performative.** Somali words dropped in for flavour rather than because
  they are the natural word. "Culturally fluent" copy that a real person
  finds embarrassing.
- **Exaggerated.** Claiming the eleven "decide a Somali marriage" when the
  person says three of them are not real issues for anyone they know.
- **Gender-inverted.** A man told to wait for her to approach his family;
  a woman told to send her people. Anything that asks one side to play the
  other's part.
- **Wrong about the family.** A script that would get someone in trouble
  with their actual wali or hooyo, said the way it is written.
- **A Somali sentence that makes a native speaker wince.** Whatever the
  reason. That line goes back behind the gate that night
  (`src/data/somali.ts`, `approved: false`).
- **False in fact.** Anything about the aroos, mahr, qabiil, going back, or a
  second wife that the person says is simply not how it works.

## WHAT COUNTS AS REAL VALUE

Evidence that something changed in the person, not that they enjoyed the
screens. In descending order of weight:

1. **A conversation happened that would not have** — Thursday's answer, with
   a specific topic and what came back. The only outcome that finally counts.
2. **They sent the eleven, or the words, to the real person**, in the session
   or by Thursday.
3. **They read the words aloud, unprompted**, in the session — trying them
   on. Or they rewrote the words into their own voice on the spot; the
   rewrite is the product's next copy.
4. **They named something new**: "I hadn't thought about that" with a
   specific topic or question, especially one of the eleven they had not
   raised with the person they are talking to.
5. **"I don't know my own answer yet"** chosen on an eleven topic, followed
   by them talking about it — the product surfaced an unexamined question.
6. **They tied it to a specific past event**: "If I'd asked that in March…"
   with a real story, not a general "this would have helped".
7. **They asked for the link for a specific named situation** — a sister, a
   friend, "my cousin who's talking to someone" — unprompted.
8. **They pushed back on the substance** of a question or a script, arguing
   with what it said rather than how it looked. Engagement with the content
   is value even when it is disagreement.
9. **They changed a plan** in front of the founder: "I'm going to ask him
   about that this weekend."

## WHAT COUNTS AS POLITE PRAISE AND SHOULD BE IGNORED

Anything with no specific referent, or addressed to the founder rather than
to their own situation. Write it down once as "polite praise" and give it
zero weight.

- "This is really nice." "It's beautiful." "I love the design." "It looks
  professional." Anything about how it looks.
- "The community needs this." "This is so needed." "Wallahi this is a good
  idea." — an opinion about other people.
- "I would definitely use this." "I'll tell everyone." — a "would", not a
  "did". Weight zero until Thursday.
- "You've done a great job." "Mashallah." — about the founder.
- Agreement with the founder's framing after the founder has said anything
  about the product. (Another reason to say nothing.)
- Enthusiasm that appears only after section D, once they know the founder
  wrote the words.
- Any praise from a person who did not reach the result screen.

The inverse rule: **complaints are never polite.** Every complaint is
classified as bug, confusion, or cultural failure, and none is discarded.

## PRECOMMITTED DECISION RULES

Written before session one, so the results cannot move them. Counts are out
of the sessions that reached the relevant screen.

**The one outcome.**
- **Three or more of the ten** report on Thursday a specific conversation
  that happened, which they attribute to the read or the eleven → the
  decision-layer hypothesis is supported. The eleven and the read lead the
  next pass and the posts.
- **One or two** → real but weak. The next pass is about the words and the
  moment of sending, not about features.
- **Zero** → Niyyah is a reading people finish and set down. Nothing new is
  built until the founder understands why the words were not said. The
  candidates are already on this list: they would not say them as written
  (question 7), they did not trust it (15), or the counterpart was never
  reachable.

**The eleven's send.**
- At least **three of the six** currently talking say they would send it
  *and* at least **one does** by Thursday → the two-sided loop is
  real; it becomes the lead instrument in every post.
- Nobody sends, in session or by Thursday → the "Ask them" moment is the
  product's largest unkept promise. What they typed at question 10 and said
  at question 12 rewrites that screen before session eleven.

**Specificity** (`docs/RESEARCH.md` conviction 1, both rules carried as
written): **fewer than four in ten** land in "it didn't understand our
situation" → specificity is a cost the product is paying for the founder's
comfort. **Six or more** in "nobody there" → the need is density, which a
product with no pool does not meet, and the expansion question moves to
now. And question 11: if most say a non-Somali friend would need all eleven,
the content is portable and the brand is a label on an institution.

**The men.** If both talking men and the ended man describe the read as
written for a woman, or cannot name what they would use it *for* → stop
treating his read as done because it mirrors hers; the men's side is designed
from their answers or not at all (the men's gap in `docs/RESEARCH.md`).

**Cultural failure.** Any line two people independently call outsider,
auntie-ish, performative, exaggerated or gender-inverted is changed or
removed before the next session — copy only, no logic. Any Somali line one
native speaker winces at goes back behind the gate that night. This is the
one kind of change permitted mid-sprint besides a bug.

**"Would you say the words."** If **fewer than half** who reached the result
would say the words as written, and their rewrites share a direction (too
formal, too long, too English, too soft) → the scripts are rewritten in
that direction after session ten, from their rewrites.

**The guide** (`docs/RESEARCH.md` A3 stays as written). If nobody opens it
unprompted in ten sessions, that is recorded and A3's readout rule stands;
nothing changes on ten.

**Willingness to pay** (A5, and `docs/RESEARCH.md` conviction 7 as written):
**fewer than three in ten** can name a payment at a family wedding, with an
amount → the belief that families pay at the nikah stays class F, and the
call after the joint view (formerly "Deciding together", `docs/PRODUCT.md`)
is re-examined as the first product rather than a fee paid at the nikah.
Seven or more name one → the cultural half is attested. Neither is a price;
no price is decided on ten.

**The connectors** (`docs/RESEARCH.md` conviction 12): recruiting these ten
runs through the ten connectors. **Fewer than three unconditional yeses** to
"would you post it into the room this month" → the network channel does not
exist, and the pivot to mosque young-adult circles happens now, not at week
eight. Record yes / no / condition in `docs/RESEARCH.md` beside the sessions.

**On the three-bin thresholds.** `docs/RESEARCH.md` sets two: fewer than four
in ten in "didn't understand our situation" (conviction 1) and six or more in
"nobody there" (the fatal-three restatement). Both are carried above as
written. At ten people either firing is a finding; neither firing is not a
pass — it is "specificity unproven", and the posts' via split decides it.

**What the founder will not do**, whatever anyone says: implement any feature
suggested in a session before all ten are done; change any copy mid-sprint
except under the two rules above; touch the map; rebuild anything cut on
2026-09-24; recruit an eleventh before the ten are logged.

## THE ONE OUTCOME, RESTATED

**Did Niyyah cause a conversation that otherwise probably would not have
happened?** Counted on Thursday, in the person's words, with the topic named,
answered "we talked" — the same answer that flips `followed-through` in the
product, and the second half of `docs/PRODUCT.md`'s own activation
definition ("reached one instrument's words within the first session, and
answered one follow-up within fourteen days"). Everything else in this
protocol exists to explain that number — not to substitute for it.
