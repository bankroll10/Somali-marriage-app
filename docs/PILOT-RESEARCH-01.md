# Pilot research 01: would they use the introduction service?

*Prepared 2026-09-27. No interview has taken place and nobody has been
contacted. It tests MEET (the introduction made by hand, `docs/OPS.md`
runbook); `docs/PROTOCOL.md` tests the instruments.*

The question: would Somali Muslim adults seeking marriage take part in an
introduction made through Niyyah, and on what terms of trust, privacy and
control? Founder-facilitated introductions are an unvalidated operating
experiment. This round learns what people did recently and what they say
about three ways an introduction could work. It is not proof of demand.

## 1. The first round

Six conversations, about 20 minutes each, in person or on a call: three women
and three men, 18 or older, seeking marriage now, and connected to
Minneapolis–St. Paul (living there, or with family and community there). At
least three from outside the founder's close friends: a friend's friend,
someone from a connector's room. Not first-degree family, not anyone the
founder has been romantically connected to. The two women already on the list
are not recruited through this packet (`docs/DECISIONS.md` Part 22).

Six people is a small qualitative sample. It can show recurring reasons,
stalls and objections, not market size or a conversion rate. In
`docs/RESEARCH.md` it earns class D at most, with n = 6 beside every claim.

## 2. Recruitment message

Copy as is. Say nothing more about the product, and send no link.

> Salaam. I'm working on something for Somali people who want to get married,
> and before I go further I want to hear what finding someone has actually
> been like. Would you give me 20 minutes, in person or on a call? It's a
> conversation about your experience, not a sign-up: nothing to join, and you
> don't have to share anything private. I take notes with no name on them, and
> you can stop whenever you like. If a day this week or next works, tell me
> which.

## 3. Interview script

Say first: *"I'll ask about what you've actually done, not what you'd do. I
don't need anyone's name, and you can skip anything. Notes, no recording."*
Follow-ups are "what happened next?" and "who was that, by role?", never a
suggestion.

1. Think of the most recent time you actually tried to meet someone for
   marriage. What did you do first, and roughly when?
2. Who helped, or who knew? What did each of them do?
3. What did you share about yourself, and with whom? What did you learn about
   the other person before deciding anything?
4. *(optional)* Was there a point that felt uncomfortable, or that you didn't
   trust? What was it about?
5. Where did it stop, and what stopped it?
6. What did you do next, or what have you tried since?
7. *(optional)* Is there anything you would never want shared, or anyone you
   would never want to know you were looking?
8. Has anyone ever offered to introduce you to someone? What happened, and
   what did you decide?

If a name or an identifying story about a third person starts, say "you don't
need to tell me who" and write down the role only.

## 4. Three concept cards

Shown only after question 8, all three in the same voice: *"Here are three
ways an introduction could work. None is a promise."* If asked afterwards, say
A is closest to what the founder is trying by hand and B and C are ideas, not
things that exist. Rotate the order, one per conversation: ABC, BCA, CAB, ACB,
BAC, CBA.

**A. One person speaks with everyone.** A person running the service talks
with you, checks who you are, speaks with one person who knows you, writes a
short description with you that does not say who you are, and proposes one
introduction at a time. Nothing identifying is shared until both say yes.
Tradeoffs: one person's judgment; you must speak with and trust them; they
know you are looking; slow.

**B. You review a few, privately (a possible concept).** You approve a short
description of yourself. Now and then you privately see a small set, say
three, of others' descriptions and choose whether to go further. They do the
same. Contact is exchanged only when both choose. Tradeoffs: more choice, less
waiting; more people see your description; nobody has spoken with the other
person for you; it can feel like a list.

**C. Someone you choose carries it (a possible concept).** You name a person
you trust, an aunt, an imam, a friend, who receives the descriptions, speaks
for you and relays. You still decide. Tradeoffs: someone you already trust;
they learn you are looking and see what you see; it depends on their time and
judgment; it may bring family in earlier than you want.

For each card: What concerns you? What would you change? What would make you
say no to it? After all three: which, if any, would you try first, and what
stops you? Weigh that last answer lightly; it is a stated preference.

## 5. Control and consent

Ask each plainly; record the answer in their words.

- **The description.** Write it yourself, approve it word for word, or have
  someone write it? What must never be in it?
- **When identity crosses.** Nothing identifying until both say yes: right
  moment, too early, too late? First name, number, photo, workplace: which,
  and when?
- **The reference.** One person who knows you would be spoken with first.
  Would that be all right? Who would you never want called?
- **The founder's involvement.** One Somali person from the community runs
  this. More likely to take part, or less? What would you need to know about
  them? Would you rather only see descriptions and never speak with anyone?
- **Family.** Who, if anyone, should know before an introduction? After?
  Never? Assume nothing; the answers will differ.
- **Leaving.** If you wanted out midway, what should happen to what you
  shared?

## 6. Evidence template

One note per conversation, kept outside this repository in an access-controlled
place, under a label only (P1 to P6, with W or M), never a name, phone or
workplace. No audio by default; if a participant asks to be recorded, that is
their choice, and the file goes once the note is written. Only a paraphrased
Feedback entry, in `docs/RESEARCH.md`'s template, is ever committed.

```markdown
## P<n> (<W|M>) · <date> · <in person | call> · <close circle | outside it>
Connection to the metro: <lives there | family there | other>
Recent attempt: what · who helped (roles) · shared what, with whom · stall · next
Discomfort or distrust: <paraphrased>
Concepts (order shown: <…>): A concern/change/reject · B … · C …
Control and consent: description · identity timing · reference · founder · family · leaving
Stated preference: <which, if any, and what stops them>
Concrete actions: <asked for the link | asked when it starts | named a reference | offered a friend | none>
Researcher uncertainty: <what I may have led, misread, or did not ask>
```

## 7. Decision guide

Read the six notes together. Keep two columns apart: what people **said**
(compliments, "I'd probably try it", a preferred card) and what people **did**
(a real recent attempt that stalled at a step the process answers; asked for
the link or a start date; named a reference; offered a friend). Only the
second column is evidence of willingness. Six conversations give no
percentage; write what was said, by how many, and what remains uncertain.

- **Try manual introductions** (the tabletop drill, then screening) when
  several recent attempts stalled at something the runbook answers (no
  trusted go-between, information leaking, no private way to say no), the
  concerns about A are ones the runbook already meets, and some people took a
  concrete action.
- **Modify the process first** when people are willing but object to one
  step: the reference call, speaking with the founder, what the description
  holds, when contact crosses, or family timing. Change that step in
  `docs/OPS.md`, record why in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, drill again.
- **Test a different approach** when reactions favour B or C for reasons
  about control rather than novelty, or people already have an intermediary
  and want no founder in the loop. Design the smallest test by hand; build
  nothing.
- **Pause recruitment** when the recent attempts show no stall this service
  addresses, distrust centres on one person from the community knowing who is
  looking, nobody takes a concrete action, or what women and men ask for
  cannot be met by one process. That is a finding, written down as one.

The reading goes into `docs/RESEARCH.md` as dated Feedback entries and a line
under A10, and the decision into `docs/DECISIONS.md`, before anything is built
or anyone is recruited.
